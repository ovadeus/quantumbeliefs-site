What you’ll learn

This program teaches the framework from Quantum Beliefs. You’ll learn how a belief forms, how it’s held, and how it can be read with care, using the Cognitive Qubit, its three dials (amplitude, phase, and coherence), and The Belief Genome Color Band.

By the end of the program you’ll be able to:

  1. Explain why a single survey answer hides most of what a belief is, and what the Collapse Gap reveals.
  2. Explain why this framework is “math, not physics.”
  3. Describe how the brain makes and keeps a belief.
  4. Use the Cognitive Qubit and its three dials to describe how a belief is held.
  5. Read amplitude on the 1 to 9 scale, where 5 means uncommitted (in superposition), not neutral.
  6. Recognize how framing, order, and wording change an answer.
  7. Read coherence on the A to E scale.
  8. Describe collapse, entanglement, and scars, with care and without diagnosis.
  9. Explain how the entropy slider and its seven channels read the gap between question and answer.
  10. Read a Belief Genome across 124 dimensions, describe BGTP, and speak honestly about the ethics of reading belief.

This course does not claim that your brain is a quantum computer. It borrows mathematical tools from quantum theory — the field of quantum cognition — to describe how people judge and decide.

Course outline

Ten modules, plus a final exam and capstone. Each module has a narrated lesson, a lesson page, a workbook, a hands-on exercise, and a quiz. The course is sequential: each module opens when you’ve finished the one before it. These short previews are from the official module promises.

You start with a short Start Here section: a welcome, the program overview, and how the course works.

How the course works

  • Each module ends with a 10-question quiz. You need 70% (7 of 10) to pass and open the next module. Unlimited retakes.
  • The final exam is 50 questions. You need 80% (40 of 50) to pass, with 3 attempts, then a 24-hour wait before you can try again.
  • The capstone is accepted on submission when you check your work against the published rubric.
